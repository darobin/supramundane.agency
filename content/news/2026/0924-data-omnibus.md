Simplification is a complicated project. The instinct behind the Draghi, Letta, and Niinistö reports is
sound — Europe’s regulatory architecture has accumulated complexity faster than it has delivered value —
and the Commission’s Digital Omnibus is meant to answer it. But good regulation is not an obstacle to
competitiveness; it is one of its preconditions. Markets work when they are a trusted, reliable
environment with legible rules and predictable enforcement, and where that environment is missing they
default to the incumbents who can absorb uncertainty as a cost of doing business.

*A Real Digital Omnibus for European Competitiveness and Security* goes through the data aspects of the
proposal with that test in mind. A small number of provisions achieve genuine simplification and make the
rules more reliable; a larger number, however well-intentioned, would produce outcomes contrary to the
Commission’s own stated aims. The report gives trilogue interlocutors the framework and background to
tell which provisions to retain, which to improve, and which to reconsider.
