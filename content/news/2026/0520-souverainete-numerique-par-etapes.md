French Response, the X account run by the Quai d’Orsay to answer disinformation with satire, has earned
plenty of praise. In a [tribune in *Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/idees/article/2026/05/20/la-souverainete-numerique-se-construit-par-etapes-et-si-les-institutions-ne-bougent-pas-le-public-ne-bougera-pas_6691743_3232.html),
Nicolas Hénin and I argue that it is a tactical response, not a strategic one: it deals with content, not
channels. As long as government communication keeps favouring — and legitimising — a platform that openly
interferes in French and European politics, it will be stuck rebutting the lies it helps amplify. The
state should instead invest in healthy, well-governed digital platforms: digital sovereignty is built in
steps, and if institutions don’t move, the public won’t either. (In French.)
